# Plan

For Mediterranean chlorophyll, MY and NRT use the same processing chain for the 1 km reflectances and the same regional chlorophyll algorithms. However, MY uses consolidated or reprocessed inputs, while NRT uses the inputs and ancillary information available at processing time. Consequently, their values—and sometimes valid-pixel coverage—can differ. Copernicus ocean-colour manual, pp. 20–21
The distinction is:
Stage Data to use What it establishes
Historical training MY L3 inputs → future MY L3 CHL targets Whether historical observations contain useful forecasting signals.
Operational validation NRT inputs actually available at each forecast’s issue time → future observed L3 CHL Whether the model works with the data it will receive in production. Targets can be evaluated later using consolidated MY observations.
Daily forecasting Latest available NRT L3 observations Forecast CHL for tomorrow, +4 or +7 days. MY is too delayed to supply the latest inputs.

Training and testing exclusively on MY would therefore be insufficient evidence for operational performance. It could give optimistic results because the historical inputs have benefited from processing unavailable when a real forecast would have been issued. Merely shifting MY timestamps to simulate publication delays does not reproduce those NRT differences.
My recommendation is to:

1. Build the initial model using MY L3 for its long historical record.
2. Obtain historical NRT where available and start retaining daily NRT downloads.
3. Compare MY and NRT over overlapping dates in your area: CHL differences, missingness and actual forecast performance.
4. Fine-tune or retrain with NRT inputs if the comparison shows a meaningful loss of performance.
   For example, Mediterranean NRT CHL has a documented delivery target of 20:00 UTC on the day after acquisition. A forecast issued before that delivery must use older CHL observations, even if newer SST or wind is available. Copernicus delivery schedule, Table 9
   MY remains useful for training and eventual verification; NRT is needed for live inputs and a credible operational test. Both can remain L3 with gaps, consistent with your objective.
